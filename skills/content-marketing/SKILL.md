# Content Marketing

Publish fewer, better pieces that answer the questions buyers actually ask,
then distribute each one deliberately and measure whether it moves a business
outcome. Content without a distribution plan or a measurable goal is a cost,
not an asset.

## When to use

- "What should we write about?", "build a content strategy / calendar".
- Writing a brief for a writer, an agency or a subagent.
- Turning one asset into many (repurposing).
- "Is our blog working?": content performance review, refresh or pruning.

## When not to use

- Technical SEO (crawling, indexing, Core Web Vitals): `seo-audit`.
- Writing a single developer tutorial or engineering post: `technical-blogging`.
- Line-level copy for pages and ads: `copywriting`.
- Short-form social calendars: `social-media`.
- Recurring editorial newsletter: `newsletters`.

## Workflow

1. **Define audience, stage and goal.** Name the ideal customer profile, the awareness stage you are targeting (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware) and the business goal (signups, demo requests, activation, retention).

   **Check:** One sentence states who the content is for and which metric it should move.

2. **Research topics from real questions.** Mine support tickets, sales call notes, community threads, Search Console queries and keyword tools. Classify each topic by intent: informational, commercial investigation, transactional or navigational. Delegate source mining to subagents in parallel (one per source), each returning a de-duplicated list of questions with counts.

   **Check:** The topic list records the source and intent for every topic.

3. **Group into pillars and clusters.** Choose three to five pillars; each gets one comprehensive pillar page and cluster pieces that link to it and to each other. Score topics by business relevance, demand and your ability to say something original (1-5 each) and rank.

   **Check:** The top ten topics each have a pillar, an intent and a score.

4. **Write a brief per piece** (template below). A brief is the contract between strategy and writer, human or agent.

   **Check:** The brief names the reader, the question answered, the angle and the call to action.

5. **Produce and review.** Draft from the brief, then a subject-matter review, an edit and a fact-check: every statistic and claim gets a source link, or is removed. Add what only you can add: original data, real examples, screenshots, expert quotes. Never mass-produce thin pages; search engines treat scaled low-value content as spam regardless of how it was made.

   **Check:** Every factual claim has a source, and the piece contains at least one element competitors cannot copy.

6. **Plan distribution before publishing.** For each piece list owned channels (newsletter, social, community, product), earned channels (partners, communities, press) and any paid amplification. Publishing and posting happen only after explicit user confirmation.

   **Check:** Each piece has at least three distribution actions with owners and dates.

7. **Repurpose** using the table below, adapting to each format rather than pasting.

   **Check:** Each core asset has a derivative plan in the calendar.

8. **Measure, refresh and prune quarterly.** Compare each piece against its goal. Refresh pieces with falling traffic but proven intent, merge overlapping pieces, and redirect or remove pages that serve no reader.

   **Check:** The quarterly review lists refresh, merge and remove decisions.

## Brief template

```markdown
# Brief: <working title>
- Reader: <role, situation, awareness stage>
- Question this answers: <the query or problem in the reader's words>
- Search intent / primary query: <intent, query, related queries>
- Angle: <what we say that others do not; our evidence>
- Outline: <H2s as questions or steps>
- Sources and SMEs: <links, people to interview>
- Internal links: <pillar page, related pieces>
- Call to action: <one next step>
- Format and length: <guide, comparison, checklist, video; target length set by intent>
- Distribution: <channels, owners, dates>
- Success metric: <metric and target window>
```

## Repurposing

| Core asset | Derivatives |
| :--- | :--- |
| Long-form guide | Checklist download, social carousel, newsletter section, short video walkthrough |
| Webinar or talk | Edited recording, blog recap, clips, slide document |
| Original research | Report, charts for social, press pitch, sales one-pager |
| Customer story | Case study page, quote cards, sales deck slide |
| Podcast episode | Show notes, audiogram clips, quote posts, article |

## Measurement

| Layer | Metrics |
| :--- | :--- |
| Reach | Impressions and clicks in Search Console, sessions by channel, rankings for target queries |
| Engagement | Engaged time, scroll depth, return visits, newsletter signups from the piece |
| Outcome | Signups, demo requests or activations that first-touched or assisted from content; self-reported "how did you hear about us" |

Attribution undercounts content: readers often return later through direct or branded search. Read self-reported attribution alongside analytics and judge pieces over months, not days.

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Do instead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pick topics by keyword volume alone | Start from real customer questions and intent | High-volume topics you cannot win, or that never convert, waste effort |
| Publish and hope | Plan distribution per piece | Most content gets little organic reach at launch |
| Mass-generate thin pages | Fewer pieces with original insight | Search engines demote scaled low-value content |
| Unsourced statistics | Link every claim or remove it | Fabricated or stale numbers destroy credibility |
| Judge content on week-one traffic | Review over a quarter with assisted conversions | Search and word of mouth take time |
| Let old posts rot | Refresh, merge or remove quarterly | Outdated pages mislead readers and dilute the site |

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Audience, awareness stage and goal metric are written down.
- [ ] Topics come from real questions, with intent and score recorded.
- [ ] Every piece has a brief, an SME review and a fact-check.
- [ ] Every piece has a distribution plan; nothing was published without explicit user confirmation.
- [ ] Measurement covers reach, engagement and outcome.
- [ ] A quarterly refresh and prune review is scheduled.
