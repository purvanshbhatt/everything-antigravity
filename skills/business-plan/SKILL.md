# Business Plan

A business plan is an argument: this problem matters, these customers will
pay, this is how we reach them, and this is what it costs and when it pays
back. Build it from a canvas and evidence, link every number to a model, and
tailor depth to the reader. It is not financial or legal advice; lenders,
investors and regulators may need review by an accountant or lawyer.

## When to use

- A business plan for a loan, grant, investor or partner.
- An internal strategy or go-to-market document for a new product or market.
- An annual plan for a board.
- Turning a rough idea into a structured plan with milestones and risks.

## When not to use

- Investor slides: `pitch-deck` (the plan can feed it).
- The financial model itself: `financial-modeling`, `unit-economics`.
- Market sizing in depth: `market-research`.
- Grant-specific narrative and budgets: `grant-writing`.
- Quarterly team goals: `okrs`.

## Workflow

1. **Fix the audience and format.** Lender, investor, grant body, board or internal team. Get any required template, page limits and evaluation criteria first; banks and grant programs often prescribe sections.

   **Check:** The outline mirrors the reader's required sections and length.

2. **Fill a canvas first.** Use the Business Model Canvas (nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure) or, for an early idea, the Lean Canvas (problem, customer segments, unique value proposition, solution, channels, revenue streams, cost structure, key metrics, unfair advantage). Mark each block `evidence` or `assumption`.

   **Check:** Every block is filled, and the riskiest assumptions are highlighted.

3. **Gather evidence.** Customer interviews and quotes, pilot or waitlist data, letters of intent, market sizing with sources (`market-research`), and the competitive landscape (`competitive-analysis`). Never fabricate traction, testimonials or market figures; use placeholders until real data exists.

   **Check:** Each claim in the plan cites evidence or is labelled an assumption.

4. **Write the go-to-market.** Ideal customer profile, positioning, pricing and packaging, channels, sales motion (self-serve, sales-led, partner-led), funnel targets with conversion assumptions, and a first-90-days plan.

   **Check:** Funnel targets multiply out to the revenue in the financial model.

5. **Describe operations and team.** Key activities, suppliers and partners, systems, licences, permits and regulatory requirements, the current team and hires by milestone.

   **Check:** Every hire in the plan appears in the model's cost lines with a start date.

6. **Link the financial summary to the model.** Show revenue build, gross margin, operating costs, cash runway, funding need and use of funds, with base, downside and upside scenarios. Every number in the document traces to a cell in the model (Google Sheets or a spreadsheet file).

   **Check:** Spot-check five numbers from the document against the model; all match.

7. **Set milestones and risks.** Milestones have dates and measurable criteria. The risk register (format below) covers market, product, execution, financial, regulatory and key-person risk.

   **Check:** Every high-scoring risk has an owner and a mitigation.

8. **Write, then red-team.** Write the one-page executive summary last. Ask a subagent to critique the plan as a sceptical reader of this audience type, listing the weakest claims and missing evidence; fix or acknowledge each. Share externally only after explicit user confirmation.

   **Check:** Every critique item is fixed or acknowledged in the plan, and numbers are consistent across sections.

## Plan outline

| Section | Contents |
| :--- | :--- |
| Executive summary | Problem, solution, customer, traction, ask, one page |
| Problem and customer | Who has the problem, evidence, current alternatives |
| Solution and product | What it does, why now, roadmap stages |
| Market | TAM, SAM, SOM with sources and formulas |
| Competition | Alternatives, positioning, defensibility |
| Go-to-market | ICP, pricing, channels, sales motion, funnel targets |
| Operations | Activities, partners, systems, compliance requirements |
| Team | Founders, key hires, advisers, gaps |
| Milestones | Dated, measurable, tied to funding stages |
| Financials | Summary tables, scenarios, funding need, use of funds |
| Risks | Register with mitigations |
| Appendix | Model link, research, letters of intent |

## Risk register

| Risk | Category | Likelihood 1-5 | Impact 1-5 | Score | Mitigation | Owner | Early warning signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| <cause and effect> | Market / Product / Execution / Financial / Regulatory / People | 3 | 4 | 12 | <action> | <name> | <metric or event> |

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Do instead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Start writing prose | Canvas and evidence first | Prose hides missing logic |
| "If we get 1% of a huge market" | Bottom-up sizing and funnel math | Top-down share claims are not a plan |
| Hard-coded numbers in the document | Link every number to the model | Numbers drift out of sync across sections |
| Only an optimistic case | Base, downside and upside scenarios | Readers test the downside first |
| Invented traction or quotes | Real data or labelled placeholders | Fabrication destroys credibility and can be fraud |
| Hide risks | A register with mitigations | Acknowledged risks build trust; hidden ones are found anyway |
| One plan for every reader | Tailor to the audience's criteria | A lender and a VC read for different things |

## Verification checklist

- [ ] The outline matches the audience's required format.
- [ ] Every canvas block is marked evidence or assumption.
- [ ] No fabricated traction, quotes or market figures.
- [ ] Every number traces to the model, and scenarios exist.
- [ ] Milestones are dated and measurable; risks have owners and mitigations.
- [ ] A red-team critique was addressed.
- [ ] The plan was shared externally only after explicit user confirmation, with a note that it is not financial or legal advice.
