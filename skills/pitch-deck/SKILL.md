# Pitch Deck

A pitch deck tells one story: a painful problem, a credible solution, evidence
it is working, and what the money will achieve. Every number must be true,
defined and traceable; every slide headline states its takeaway.

## When to use

- "Help me build a pitch deck", "review my deck", "what slides do I need for a seed round?".
- Rewriting a deck after investor feedback or a failed round.
- Turning a business plan or metrics sheet into an investor narrative.
- Preparing the appendix and data room that follow a first meeting.

## When not to use

- The underlying financial model: `financial-modeling`.
- Ongoing communication with existing investors: `investor-updates`.
- General presentation craft for non-fundraising talks: `presentation-slides`.
- Securities law, instruments and terms (SAFEs, priced rounds, solicitation rules): a qualified lawyer.

## Workflow

1. **Write the one-line ask.** "Raising *<amount>* on a *<instrument>* to reach *<milestone>* by *<date>*." Note stage, target investor type and timeline. Fundraising is regulated; the user should confirm instrument, terms and how they may advertise the round with counsel.

   **Check:** The ask names an amount, a milestone and a date.

2. **Write the narrative as headlines first.** In a Doc, write one sentence per slide that states its takeaway. Fix the story before any design work.

   **Check:** Reading only the headlines tells the full story in under two minutes.

3. **Assemble slides in a sensible order** (table below). Move the strongest evidence earlier: strong traction goes right after the problem; a rare team goes near the front at pre-seed.

   **Check:** The main deck has roughly 10-15 slides; detail lives in an appendix.

4. **Define and source every metric.** Use consistent definitions (ARR versus revenue run rate, what "active" means), show time series rather than single points, and footnote definitions. Never invent or round up traction, logos, quotes or pipeline. Logos and quotes need customer permission.

   **Check:** Each number on a slide links to a cell in the metrics sheet or data room.

5. **Size the market from the bottom up.** Target customers multiplied by realistic annual contract value, with sources; show TAM, SAM and SOM and the assumptions (`market-research`).

   **Check:** The SOM calculation is visible, with sources for customer counts and price.

6. **Show competition honestly.** Compare on criteria buyers use, including the status quo and building in-house. Say why you win and where you do not.

   **Check:** The slide names alternatives that real buyers mention.

7. **Design for two versions.** A sent version that reads without a presenter (fuller headlines, footnotes) and a presented version with less text. One message per slide, large readable type, labelled charts, consistent colours. Build in Google Slides and export a PDF for sending.

   **Check:** A colleague who has not heard the pitch can explain each slide's point from the sent version.

8. **Red-team it.** Delegate a review to a subagent acting as a sceptical investor: the ten hardest questions, the weakest slides and any inconsistent numbers. Prepare answers and add appendix slides where needed.

   **Check:** Every hard question has an appendix slide or a prepared answer.

9. **Prepare the data room.** Financial model, cap table, incorporation and key contracts, metrics with definitions, product demo, team bios. Share the deck and data room only after the user confirms each recipient; use view-only links with access tracking where possible.

   **Check:** The data room index is complete, and every share was confirmed by the user.

## Slide order

| # | Slide | Headline should answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Title | What the company does, in one line |
| 2 | Problem | Who has the problem, how painful, what evidence |
| 3 | Solution | How the product removes the pain |
| 4 | Why now | What changed that makes this possible or urgent |
| 5 | Product | The key workflow, shown (screenshots or a short demo) |
| 6 | Traction | The growth and retention trend that proves demand |
| 7 | Market | The bottom-up size of the opportunity |
| 8 | Business model | How you make money and the unit economics |
| 9 | Go-to-market | How you acquire customers, with evidence |
| 10 | Competition | Why buyers choose you over the alternatives |
| 11 | Team | Why this team will win |
| 12 | Financials | Projection summary and the key assumptions |
| 13 | Ask and use of funds | The amount and the milestones it reaches |
| - | Appendix | Detail, definitions, FAQ answers |

## Emphasis by stage

| Stage | Investors look for |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pre-seed | Founder insight into the problem, team fit, early signs of pull |
| Seed | Early traction, retention or engagement signals, a believable path to repeatable sales |
| Series A | Repeatable go-to-market, healthy cohort retention, unit economics that improve with scale |

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Do instead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "If we get 1% of a huge market" | Size from customers times price, with sources | Top-down sizing signals no plan to reach customers |
| Lead with vanity metrics (cumulative signups, pageviews) | Show revenue, active usage and retention over time | Cumulative charts always go up and investors discount them |
| Claim "no competitors" | Show the real alternatives, including doing nothing | It reads as not understanding the market |
| Use undefined labels ("users", "ARR") | Footnote every definition | Inconsistent definitions surface in diligence and cost trust |
| Fill slides with paragraphs | One message per slide; detail in the appendix | Investors skim |
| Show a hockey stick with no driver | Tie each inflection to a named driver and assumption | Unexplained growth curves are ignored |
| Show logos or quotes without permission | Get written approval | Unapproved use can breach customer contracts |

## Verification checklist

- [ ] The one-line ask has an amount, milestone and date.
- [ ] The headlines alone tell the story.
- [ ] Every metric is defined and traceable to source data; nothing is invented or inflated.
- [ ] Market sizing is bottom-up with sources.
- [ ] Competition includes the status quo.
- [ ] Hard questions have prepared answers.
- [ ] Each share of the deck or data room was confirmed by the user; counsel reviewed instrument and terms.
