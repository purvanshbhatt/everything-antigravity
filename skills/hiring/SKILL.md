# Hiring

Hire against a written definition of success, ask every candidate the same
job-related questions, and score evidence rather than impressions. Structure
makes hiring both more predictive and fairer. Employment law differs by
country, state and city, so this skill is not legal advice: involve HR or
employment counsel for policies, offers and anything with legal exposure.

## When to use

- Opening a role: scorecard, job post, interview loop and timeline.
- Writing interview questions, rubrics or a take-home assignment.
- Running a debrief or comparing finalists.
- Auditing an existing process for inconsistency or bias.

## When not to use

- Onboarding, 1:1s and performance reviews after the offer: `people-operations`.
- Handbook rules, background-check or recruiting policies: `policy-drafting`, then counsel.
- Headcount cost and budget scenarios: `financial-modeling`.
- Outreach sequences to passive candidates: follow `sales-outreach` consent and personalization rules, and confirm every send.

## Workflow

1. **Write the scorecard.** One-sentence mission, 3-5 measurable outcomes for the first 6-12 months, and the competencies (skills and behaviours) each outcome needs. Split must-haves from nice-to-haves. Drop proxies (degree, years of experience, "culture fit") unless they are genuinely required for the job.

   **Check:** Every competency traces to an outcome, and there are at most five must-haves.

2. **Set level and pay range before posting.** Several jurisdictions require pay ranges in job ads (some US states and cities, and the EU Pay Transparency Directive); check the rules for where the role and the candidates are. Many places also ban asking about salary history.

   **Check:** The range is approved and the posting rules for each location were checked.

3. **Draft the job post** in a Google Doc: what you will achieve (outcomes), what you need (must-haves only), nice to have, how the team works, pay and benefits, process and timeline, accommodation offer, equal-opportunity statement. Use plain, gender-neutral language; avoid "rockstar", "ninja" and age-coded phrases ("digital native", "young team").

   **Check:** Every requirement in the post appears in the scorecard, and the user approved the text before it is posted anywhere.

4. **Design the loop.** Map each competency to one or two stages, for example: screen (logistics, must-haves), hiring manager (outcomes and motivation), work sample (core skills), collaboration (behaviours), final. Tell candidates the total time commitment up front.

   **Check:** The competency-by-stage matrix has no gaps and no stage scores more than three competencies.

5. **Build interview kits.** For each stage: core questions (behavioural "tell me about a time" and situational), follow-up probes, and a 1-4 rubric with evidence anchors (example below). Every candidate for the role gets the same core questions. Interviewers score independently in the scorecard before any discussion.

   **Check:** Each question has anchors for every score, and the kit fits the time slot.

6. **Design the work sample.** Make it representative of real work, time-boxed (state the realistic time; keep it to a few hours), the same for everyone, and scored against criteria shared with the candidate. Never ask for unpaid production work. For longer tasks, pay for the time or offer an alternative (live session, existing portfolio).

   **Check:** A colleague completed it within the stated time using only the instructions given.

7. **Debrief and decide.** Collect independent scores first, then discuss evidence competency by competency; the hiring manager decides against the scorecard and records the rationale. "Not a fit" needs a specific, job-related reason.

   **Check:** The written rationale cites scorecard evidence for each must-have.

8. **References, offer and close.** Ask structured reference questions tied to the scorecard. Offers go through HR or counsel. Tell every candidate the outcome promptly. Never send offers, rejections or candidate messages without explicit user confirmation.

   **Check:** Every candidate in the pipeline has a recorded outcome, and candidate data is retained or deleted per policy.

## Rubric anchors (example)

Competency: diagnosing production issues.

| Score | Evidence heard |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | No specific incident; generic statements only |
| 2 | A real incident, but own role unclear and no root cause found |
| 3 | Clear personal actions, systematic hypotheses, root cause, follow-up fix |
| 4 | All of 3, plus prevention adopted by the team and knowledge shared |

## Fairness rules

- Never ask about, infer or weigh protected characteristics or proxies for them: age, race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, pregnancy or family plans, marital status, disability or health, genetic information, military or veteran status. Where the law allows, you may ask whether the candidate can perform the essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation.
- Offer accommodations at every stage and make the request easy.
- AI may draft posts, structure notes and summarize interview transcripts that candidates agreed to. It must not reject, rank or score candidates without human review. Automated hiring tools are regulated in some places (for example, New York City requires bias audits, and the EU AI Act treats recruitment AI as high-risk). Only paste candidate data into tools approved for personal data.
- With HR, review pass-through rates by stage using voluntary, separately stored demographic data. In the US, a group selection rate below four-fifths of the highest group's rate is a common screening signal for adverse impact.

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Do instead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Unstructured "chat" interviews | Same core questions and anchored rubrics | Unstructured interviews predict poorly and amplify bias |
| Debrief before scores are submitted | Independent scores first | The first or loudest opinion anchors everyone else |
| Ten-item requirement lists | Five must-haves at most | Long lists deter qualified applicants who do not tick every box |
| Unpaid multi-day take-homes | Time-boxed or paid work sample | Excludes people with less free time and invites exploitation claims |
| "Culture fit" as a reason | Name the job-related behaviour | Vague fit judgments hide similarity bias |
| Let an AI tool auto-reject | Human review of every decision | Legal exposure and unexamined bias |
| Ghost rejected candidates | Prompt, respectful outcome | Candidate experience shapes your reputation and future pipeline |

## Verification checklist

- [ ] The scorecard defines outcomes and five or fewer must-haves.
- [ ] Pay range is set and location posting rules were checked.
- [ ] Every competency is covered by the loop, with anchored rubrics.
- [ ] Interviewers scored independently before the debrief.
- [ ] No question touches protected characteristics; accommodations were offered.
- [ ] No candidate message, offer or rejection went out without explicit user confirmation.
