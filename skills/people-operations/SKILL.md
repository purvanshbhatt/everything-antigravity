# People Operations

Make the routine parts of managing people predictable: every new hire knows
what good looks like, every report gets regular attention and honest
feedback, and reviews contain no surprises. Employment rules differ by
location, so anything touching contracts, pay, discipline or termination goes
to HR or employment counsel; this skill is not legal advice.

## When to use

- Preparing onboarding for a new hire, or fixing a slow ramp-up.
- Setting up 1:1s, writing feedback, or preparing a hard conversation.
- Designing or running a performance review cycle.
- Writing remote or hybrid working norms for a team.
- Planning an offboarding.

## When not to use

- Recruiting, interviews and offers: `hiring`.
- Writing handbook or HR policies: `policy-drafting`, then counsel.
- Team goal setting at company level: `okrs`.
- Running a specific meeting or retro: `meeting-facilitation`.

## Workflow

1. **Name the moment and constraints.** Onboarding, ongoing management, review cycle, performance concern, or offboarding. Note team size, locations and time zones, existing tools (HRIS, Google Workspace, ticketing) and any existing policy.

   **Check:** You can state which section below applies and who owns the outcome.

2. **Onboarding.** Build a task-list artifact with owners and dates: preboarding (accounts, hardware, access requests, welcome message), day 1 (people, tools, first small task), week 1 (a shippable first deliverable, buddy sessions), and 30/60/90-day outcomes agreed with the manager. Store docs in a shared Drive folder linked from the plan.

   **Check:** Access requests are filed before the start date, and the new hire has a defined first-week deliverable.

3. **1:1s.** Weekly or fortnightly, 30 minutes, owned by the report, with a shared Google Doc agenda (template below). Never cancel twice in a row; reschedule.

   **Check:** Each 1:1 doc has a dated entry with actions and owners.

4. **Feedback.** Use Situation-Behaviour-Impact, then ask a question and agree a next step. Give it within days, privately when it is critical, and balance positive and corrective feedback over time.

   **Check:** The feedback names an observable behaviour, not a personality trait.

5. **Review cycle.** Publish the timeline, inputs (self-review, peer input, manager assessment) and a rating scale with written definitions. Calibrate across managers before ratings are shared. Run the bias checks below. Deliver in a conversation, then in writing.

   **Check:** Every rating cites evidence from the whole period, and calibration notes are recorded.

6. **Underperformance.** Raise it early and informally with specific examples and clear expectations, then offer support and check in on a set cadence. A formal improvement plan is drafted only with HR or counsel. The employee should never be surprised.

   **Check:** Expectations, support and dates are written down and shared with the employee.

7. **Offboarding.** Revoke access on the last day (or immediately, if needed), recover equipment, transfer knowledge and ownership of documents and systems, hold an exit conversation, and confirm final pay and benefits rules with HR for the location.

   **Check:** An access checklist is complete and every owned system has a new owner.

8. **Remote and hybrid norms.** Write them down: core overlap hours, expected response times per channel, async-first decisions in docs, meeting-free blocks, rotating meeting times across time zones, and how people signal availability.

   **Check:** Norms live in the team handbook, and new hires read them in week 1.

Do not send messages to employees, schedule meetings, or change HRIS records without explicit user confirmation. Employee data is sensitive: use the minimum needed and only in tools approved for personal data.

## Templates

**1:1 agenda (shared doc, newest entry on top)**

```markdown
## <date>
- Report's topics:
- Manager's topics:
- Progress on goals / blockers:
- Feedback (both directions):
- Actions: <owner> - <action> - <due>
```

**30/60/90 plan**

| Window | Learn | Contribute | Outcome agreed with manager |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Days 1-30 | Product, customers, codebase or processes | Small scoped tasks | <outcome> |
| Days 31-60 | Deeper domain ownership | Own a project end to end | <outcome> |
| Days 61-90 | Cross-team context | Lead or improve something | <outcome> |

**SBI example:** "In Tuesday's client call (situation), you answered the pricing question before Sam finished (behaviour). Sam lost the thread and the client asked twice (impact). What was going on for you?"

## Review bias checks

| Bias | Symptom | Countermeasure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Recency | Only last month's work cited | Keep a running evidence log; require examples across the period |
| Halo or horns | One trait colours every rating | Rate each competency separately with its own evidence |
| Similarity | Higher ratings for people like the manager | Calibration with peers; challenge vague praise |
| Central tendency or leniency | Everyone "meets expectations" or above | Written rating definitions; compare distributions across teams |
| Attribution | Credit for wins, blame for team failures | Ask what the person controlled |

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Do instead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Save feedback for the annual review | Give it within days | Late feedback cannot change outcomes and feels like an ambush |
| Feedback about traits ("not a team player") | Observable behaviour and impact | People can change behaviour, not labels |
| Onboarding by "shadow someone" | Written plan, buddy and first deliverable | Unstructured ramp-up wastes weeks |
| Start an improvement plan without HR | Involve HR or counsel first | Legal exposure and inconsistent treatment |
| Meetings always in one time zone | Rotate or go async | The same people always pay the cost |
| Store review notes in personal files | Approved HR system with access controls | Privacy, retention rules and continuity |

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Onboarding plan has owners, dates, a week-1 deliverable and 30/60/90 outcomes.
- [ ] 1:1s happen on cadence with written actions.
- [ ] Review ratings cite evidence from the full period and were calibrated.
- [ ] Performance concerns are documented and HR is involved before any formal step.
- [ ] Remote norms are written and linked in onboarding.
- [ ] No message, invite or record change happened without explicit user confirmation.
